## Platform

web

## Stack

Static: `index.html`, one stylesheet and plain ES modules, no build step and no backend (brief: "This app has no backend at all"). Hosted on GitHub Pages from the public repo `Souradip121/who-texts-first`, served from the `gh-pages` branch like `bunk` (commits on `main` are blocked on the author's machine). Anyone can fork it and enable Pages.

## Users

Indian college students (mostly CS/engineering) who follow @souradip.sh on Instagram. They arrive from an Instagram DM after commenting CRUSH on the reel, so they open the link on a phone, often inside Instagram's in-app browser. They want to know whether the person they text (a crush, usually) is putting in as much effort as they are.

## Product Purpose

Answer one question: "Who's putting in the effort in this WhatsApp chat?" Two ways:

1. **Copy the prompt.** A ready-made prompt the student pastes into ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude along with their exported chat. Free, and works with the AI they already use.
2. **Use your own API key.** The site reads the export in the browser, works out the numbers locally, and asks the chosen AI (OpenAI, Gemini or Claude) only for a one-line verdict, sending the stats plus about the last 300 messages. A run costs a few rupees.

Success is a student getting the numbers and the verdict in under a minute, then sending the result card to a friend.

## Positioning

No server and no account. The chat goes only to the AI the student picks, and only if they choose the API-key route. The numbers (median reply time, who starts chats after 6+ hours of silence, double texts, dry replies, messages after midnight) are computed on the phone, so they are exact rather than an AI's estimate.

## Operating Context

- Opened from an Instagram DM on a phone, one-handed, portrait. *(inferred)* Often inside Instagram's in-app browser, where downloads and clipboard access can be unreliable.
- WhatsApp exports come from Android (`11/10/26, 9:41 pm - Name: text`, a `.txt`) or iPhone (`[11/10/26, 9:41:02 PM] Name: text`, usually a `.zip` with `_chat.txt` inside), in either day/month order, 12- or 24-hour time.
- The reel (S2, ~34 s) shows: the "Copy prompt" tap, the WhatsApp export screen, the key field with provider chips and "Analyze", then the two reply-time bars racing and the verdict card typing out.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Route A: prompt with a Copy button and three steps (export without media; open ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude; attach the export and paste the prompt). The prompt asks for median reply time (ignoring gaps over 12 h), % of conversations started after 6+ h of silence, double texts, dry replies ("hmm", "ok", "k"), messages after midnight and a one-line playful verdict, as a short table plus the verdict. It must cope with both export formats and large files.
- Route B: provider chips (OpenAI, Gemini, Claude), masked key field. The key lives in memory or sessionStorage only and is sent only to the chosen provider's API. Time range: "Last 3 months" or "All time". The user says which name in the export is them.
- Results: reply-time bars for You and Them, "You start N% of chats", "hmm" × N, the AI verdict card, and "Share result" (a PNG made in the browser, nothing uploaded).
- Required copy: "Your key stays in your browser. Your chat goes only to the AI you pick, never to us."
- Errors to handle kindly: a bad key, no quota or credit, rate limits, network failure, an unknown model.
- Web only. No Android app, no analytics, no storage beyond the tab.

## Brand Commitments

Made by @souradip.sh (Instagram). Voice: casual, student-to-student, a little cheeky, never mean about the other person. Visual direction pinned by the author: concept B, "Y2K love letter" (`builds/ui-concepts/effort/effort_b_y2k.png`): pastel pink and lavender, chunky rounded type, glossy chrome heart stickers, bubble buttons. ## Product Principles

1. Privacy you can read: say exactly what leaves the phone, and to whom.
2. Numbers first, AI for the verdict: exact local stats, with the model used only for the one-liner.
3. Two routes, no dead ends: anyone without a key can still copy the prompt.
4. Screenshot-ready: every result fits on one phone screen and looks good in a reel.
5. Kind, not cruel: the verdict is playful and never insults either person.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard-reachable controls with visible focus, 4.5:1 contrast for body text, and reduced motion respected for the bar race and typing effects. *(inferred)* Many users will read English as a second language, so copy stays short and plain.
